# Improving ID-card photos for OCR: out-of-focus blur and glare removal
ECE 253 (UC San Diego, Fall 2026) project by Aaryan Garg and Myles Lazarus.
**Report:** [`report/paper.pdf`](report/paper.pdf) (ICASSP format). **Slides:** [`presentation/`](presentation/).

We treat two artifacts of hand-held ID-card photos and measure what each treatment does to OCR:
* **out-of-focus blur**: classical Wiener filter (fixed and adaptive) and Restormer (pretrained and fine-tuned);
* **glare** (specular highlights): a classical two-zone method (veil inversion + core fill) and DocSHRNet with a new
  glare-detection head (zero-shot and fine-tuned); with a video clip, multi-frame fusion;
* **both together**, in either order.

Each method is run with and without our modification and compared with the untreated image, on our 22 cards
(4 test cards never used for training or tuning) and on MIDV-2020 photos and clips (external, nothing tuned on them).
The main metric is character accuracy on fixed text boxes, read by two OCR engines (PaddleOCR and Tesseract), with
paired 95% confidence intervals; SSIM is secondary.

**Findings in one line each** (details under [Findings](#findings)):
* Fine-tuned Restormer is the only deblurring method that helps (Tesseract +0.38 on test); Wiener does not transfer to new cards.
* Whether single-image glare removal helps depends on the OCR engine: it helps Tesseract and slightly hurts PaddleOCR,
  on our test cards and on 100 MIDV photos.
* Text under clipped glare comes back only with several frames (core accuracy ~0.5 -> 0.99); reading each field from
  its best frame gets almost all of that.
* Blur + glare: deblurring does most of the work, deglare after it adds a little for Tesseract, the order does not matter.
* SSIM and OCR disagree.

## Data
* **Our photos** (`data/raw/`, not public, see Setup): 141 photos of 22 card sides (14 real cards, 8 printed
  specimens), hand-held, mostly iPhone 17, some iPhone 12 Pro. Name `{card}_{B|S}_{G|D}_{n}.png`:
  B / S = blurred (focus locked on another object) / sharp, G / D = glare / diffuse light. The reference of every image
  is the same card's sharp, diffuse photo `S_D_0`. `src/common/rectify_ids.py` finds the card, warps it to 1012 x 638
  and aligns it to the reference (ECC homography): `data/rectified/`.
* **Split** (`split.csv`, by card): 14 train / 4 val / 4 test cards. Train and val are used for training and every
  choice; test (B03, F03, F04, F11) only for the final numbers. Usable images (one glare photo is unusable):

  | | cards | reference (S_D) | blur (B_D) | glare (S_G) | blur + glare (B_G) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | train | 14 | 14 | 16 | 31 | 30 |
  | val | 4 | 4 | 4 | 8 | 8 |
  | test | 4 | 4 | 4 | 8 | 9 |

  `src/common/prepare_data.py` writes `out/pairs.csv` (glare images), `out/pairs_blur.csv` (B_D) and the split.
* **MIDV-2020 photos** (external test set; `src/common/prepare_midv.py`): the 100
  photos labelled "highlight present" (60-69 of each of 10 document types), 100 different mock documents, two phones.
  Reference = the flatbed scan of the same document; fixed boxes = the annotated fields (997 of 1341 kept: Latin
  text, >= 3 characters, horizontal); glare zones from the scan tone-matched to the photo.
* **MIDV-2020 clips** (`src/glare/prepare_midv_clips.py`): the 100 "highlight present"
  clips (90-99 of each type). Every annotated frame is aligned to one anchor frame (SIFT + RANSAC, then ECC on locally
  normalised images with the glare masked; kept only if the residual is <= 1 px): 4,641 of 6,258 frames. Two key-frame
  rules: **keymid** (the middle frame) and **keymax** (the frame with the most clipped text in the fields).
* **Privacy** (`src/common/privacy.py`): only the 8 specimen cards (B01-B03, F01-F05) are shown anywhere. Tables that
  hold card text (`results/layouts/box_layout.csv`, the per-box readings `*_boxes.csv`) keep every row and number, but
  the text of the real cards is blank in the repository; `box_ocr.py` writes the full version to `private/` (local).

## Evaluation
* **Fixed-box OCR, the main metric** (`src/common/box_ocr.py`). The line boxes PaddleOCR detects on each card's clean
  reference are used for every image of that card (they are all aligned to it); 342 of 368 boxes are labelled with
  their true text from `ground_truth/`. Each box is cropped and only recognised: PaddleOCR `TextRecognition`
  (PP-OCRv6_medium_rec) and Tesseract `--psm 7`. A box whose pixels a method leaves unchanged gets the same reading,
  so detector churn is gone.
* **Zones**, from the untreated image: **core** (clipped), **halo** (rest of the glare region: > 40 grey levels
  brighter than the reference), **outside**.
* **Score:** character accuracy = 1 - edit distance / length (lowercase letters and digits), pooled over the boxes of
  a zone. Ceiling: the reference image. Noise floor proxy (`ref_jitter`): the reference with boxes shifted by <= 2 px,
  noise and JPEG; it differs from the reference by <= 0.006 per zone for PaddleOCR.
* **Paired changes:** mean per-image difference to the untreated image with a 95% bootstrap CI over images
  (`src/common/compare_runs.py`). With 4-9 test images per condition, differences of means are not enough.
* **SSIM** to the reference: whole card, inside the glare, outside the glare (`src/common/evaluate.py`, which also
  gives whole-card PaddleOCR word F1). Do-no-harm rule for glare tuning: SSIM outside the glare may drop by <= 0.002.

## Methods
**Glare, classical** (`src/glare/classical_glare.py`, `params/glare/classical_params.json`). The halo is a white veil,
`I = R(1-a) + 255a` (a fit of glare pixels against the reference gives gain + offset/255 = 0.96 over 738 blocks).
Seeds are clipped blobs of >= 100 px; without seeds the image is returned unchanged (76 of 94 glare images have seeds).
*Our modification:* the veil is estimated by "statistics inpainting": on a 16 px grid, the local ink black level is
compared with the level expected from textured blocks far from the glare, `a = (b - b0)/(255 - b0)`, capped by the
lift of the white level, grown from the seeds, with a per-pixel background floor; then the model is inverted on RGB.
The old version used `a = a0 exp(-d/tau)` with the distance `d` to the core (`classical_params_decay.json`). Cores get
the median colour of a ring around them (flat fill); Telea inpainting is the ablation (it smears strokes into the hole).
Tried and dropped on train + val: CLAHE in the halo, an opening against laminate scratches, and a denoise before the
veil inversion (no OCR gain that transferred to test).

**Glare, learned** (`src/glare/finetune_dl.py`). The proposal's JSHDR network is no longer available, so we use
DocSHRNet (Xu et al., PRCV 2025), a NAFNet-style U-Net (34.6 M parameters) pretrained on document highlights.
*Our modification:* a detection head (4 dilated convolutions on the last decoder features) predicts a glare
probability `m`, and the output is `m*y + (1-m)*x`, so pixels not detected as glare stay as they are. Fine-tuned on the
31 train S_G pairs (256 px crops, 1500 iterations, ~1.5 h on MPS) with glare-weighted L1 + 0.2 (1 - SSIM) + 0.1 BCE of
`m` against the reference-derived glare region; checkpoint chosen on val S_G (iteration 1350:
`checkpoints/glare/docshr_mt_best.pt`). Zero-shot = the pretrained network alone.

**Glare, several frames** (`src/glare/fuse_frames.py`, MIDV clips). A pixel is glare in a frame if clipped or > 30
levels above its 25th percentile over time. Only the key frame's glare pixels are replaced, by the mean of the
frames where that pixel is clean. Baselines: the best single frame, the best frame per field (an automatic per-field
retake) and the plain temporal median.

**Blur, classical** (`src/blur/deblur_blocks/wiener.py`, `adaptive_wiener.py`). Wiener filter with a disk PSF,
`H* / (|H|^2 + K)`. Fixed: radius 6 / K 0.03 for every image, from a grid on train + val B_D
(`params/blur/wiener_params.json`; the earlier setting radius 3 / K 0.3 is run as `wiener_old`).
*Our modification (the proposal's version):* adaptive, with K from a wavelet noise estimate and the radius chosen per
image by Tesseract confidence, sharpness and ringing (`params/blur/wiener_adaptive_params.json`).

**Blur, learned** (`src/blur/finetune_deblur.py`). Restormer with the official single-image defocus weights.
*Our modification:* fine-tuned on the 16 train B_D pairs (128 px patches near text, L1 against the tone-matched sharp
photo, 2000 steps); step 1000 chosen on val (`checkpoints/blur/restormer_ours.pt`). Usage of the blocks: `src/blur/README.md`.

**Both artifacts** (`src/joint/run_chain_experiments.sh`): the frozen single methods, chained in both orders (A -> B = A first).

## Findings
1. **Deblurring:** the fine-tuned Restormer is the only method that clearly helps: Tesseract +0.38 on all four test
   cards; PaddleOCR, at 0.78 untreated, does not change significantly. Both Wiener versions helped Tesseract on the 20
   train + val images they were tuned on and not on test (one slightly blurred card drops from 0.97 to 0.21 for
   PaddleOCR), and the fixed one destroys sharp text. The network can also invent: a date read correctly untreated,
   03/07/2022, becomes 03/07/2922 (a risk for ID data).
2. **Single-image glare removal depends on the reader.** Tesseract loses most halo text to the veil (0.42 vs 0.84 on
   the reference); the fine-tuned network recovers half of that gap (+0.22). PaddleOCR already reads 0.87 of the
   untreated halo, and every method lowers it: processed strokes confuse it more than the veil. Both signs repeat
   on 100 MIDV photos and on the clips' key frames. Clipped (core) text is not recovered by any single-image method.
3. **Several frames recover clipped text:** fusion raises keymax core accuracy by +0.50 (PaddleOCR) and +0.72
   (Tesseract), close to the scan; in 99 of 100 clips every clipped pixel is clean in another frame. Reading each
   field from its best frame gets almost the same (fusion adds +0.006 / +0.013 over all fields). For an app:
   capture a short burst and ask for a retake of a clipped field, instead of inventing strokes.
4. **Both artifacts:** deblurring does most of the work (Restormer +0.09 / +0.29 on B_G); DL deglare after it adds
   +0.045 for Tesseract only; the order does not matter. On MIDV photos, chosen for glare, Restormer is nearly
   neutral and the chain inherits the engine-dependent effect of glare removal.
5. **Our modifications:** fine-tuning helps both networks on our test cards, but DocSHRNet's gain over zero-shot does
   not carry over to MIDV, whose glare is mostly textured laminate / hologram reflection; its detection head overfits
   (IoU with the glare region 0.59 on train, 0.30 on test). The statistics veil helps Tesseract only; the adaptive
   Wiener raises SSIM, not accuracy. The classical method acts only near clipped seeds and is safe but weak.
6. **SSIM and OCR disagree:** zero-shot DocSHRNet has the best whole-card SSIM (0.748 vs 0.696 untreated) partly by
   smoothing the whole card, and is the worst method for PaddleOCR in the halo. Report both.

**Limitations.** The test split has 4 cards and 4-9 images per condition, so the CIs are wide (rough for n = 4), and
several choices that looked better on train + val did not transfer. The noise floor is a proxy (one clean photo per
card). The boxes come from PaddleOCR's detector. The networks saw 31 (glare) and 16 (blur) training photos. On MIDV the
reference is a scan, so its glare zones depend on the tone matching, and only Latin fields are scored. Early blur
pilots (`docs/blur_history/`) had seen test cards, so the choice of method families was not blind; none of their
models or numbers is used.
